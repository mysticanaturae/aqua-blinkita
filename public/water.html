THE STORY OF WATER

THE OLD WAY
OF SPEAKING
TO WATER.

Before there were messages, there were shores. Before there were screens, there was the horizon.

BEFORE THE SCREEN

Before there were messages.

Before there were messages, there were shores.

Before there were screens, there was the horizon.

And people came to the water carrying things they could not carry alone.

A wish.

A prayer.

A name.

A goodbye.

A hope for someone they loved.

They whispered it toward the waves and watched the water take it away.

They believed the sea could hear.

And sometimes, they simply needed something vast enough to hold what they could no longer carry alone.

The water became a place to release.

To ask.

To remember.

To begin again.

Maybe the ancient ritual was never lost.

Maybe we simply forgot that we were still doing it.

WE STILL DO IT

We speak to the water without realizing it.

And maybe we still do this.

Without even realizing it.

We sit beside the ocean.

We watch the horizon.

We become quiet.

We think about someone.

We ask for something.

We release something.

We make a wish.

We speak to the water.

SOME WATERS ARE OLDER THAN MEMORY

The Caribbean remembers.

There is something about the Caribbean that is difficult to explain.

Perhaps it is the colour.

Perhaps it is the reef.

Perhaps it is what lies beneath the surface.

Here in Belize, the sea is not only sea.

It is filled with openings.

Caves.

Blue holes.

Hidden passages.

Places where the world above suddenly gives way to another world below.

The ancient Maya understood certain waters as sacred places — thresholds between the visible world and worlds beyond what could be seen.

THE GREAT BLUE HOLE

An opening into the unknown.

And Belize still holds one of the most extraordinary openings on Earth.

The Great Blue Hole.

From above, it looks almost impossible:

a perfect circle of deep indigo surrounded by turquoise reef.

A dark opening in the middle of an immense blue sea.

For generations, people looked into its depths and wondered what lay beneath.

Old stories described it as bottomless.

Explorers eventually descended.

They found enormous ancient stalactites deep below the surface — silent evidence of a world that existed before the sea covered it.

And still, when you look into that blue circle from above,

there is something the measurements cannot explain.

Something in the feeling.

Something in the silence.

Something in the question it leaves behind.

What else is down there?

THE STORIES OF THE REEF

And then there are the stories.

The fishermen's stories.

Stories passed quietly from one person to another.

Lights appearing where no lights should be.

Shapes beneath the surface.

Strange things seen over the reef.

Things entering the water.

Things leaving it.

Stories of places where the sea seems to behave differently.

Some people laugh.

Some people believe.

Some people simply say:

“I saw what I saw.”

I don't need to decide for you what is true.

I like the mystery.

Because the Caribbean has always had room for mystery.

It is not only a sea you can swim in.

It is a sea with stories.

A sea with memory.

A sea with depths we have mapped — and depths we have not.

WHY THE CARIBBEAN?

Because some places become part of you.

And some waters stay with you long after you leave them.

My relationship with the ocean began when I was a child.

Long before I had words for what I felt, I already knew there was something about the sea that asked me to become quiet.

Years later, I lived for three years directly on the beach in Hopkins, Belize.

There, the relationship deepened.

The ocean was no longer somewhere I went.

She was simply there.

Every day.

With the tides.

The wind.

The reef.

The changing light.

The endless blue.

I began to know her as Grandmother Ocean.

Not because I believe I can speak for her.

But because some presences are easier to listen to when we stop trying to explain them.

And perhaps this is why I became an Ocean Whisperer.

I learned to sit beside her.

To listen.

To notice what arrives in the silence.

RETURNING TO THE SHORE

I am returning to the relationship.

And now I am blessed to be returning to Hopkins.

Returning to the Caribbean shore.

Returning to a home only about fifty metres from the sea.

Back into the immediate presence of this vast, ancient consciousness I call Grandmother Ocean.

This time, I am not simply returning to the ocean.
I am returning to the relationship.

And from that shore, I can carry something for you.

Your name.

Your intention.

Your question.

The water has no borders.

You don't have to travel to the Caribbean. You can sit wherever you are and remember the old way of speaking to water.
