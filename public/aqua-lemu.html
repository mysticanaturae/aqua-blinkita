Stories of lands that disappeared.

But I know why the story stayed with me.

Because there is something about the ocean that makes us wonder what it remembers.

What lies beneath the surface.

What was here before us.

What might still exist in forms we cannot see.

THE WATER BETWEEN WORLDS

Perhaps this is why I experience the ocean differently.

When I first moved close to the Caribbean shore, something began to awaken in me.

Not because I was searching for it.

I wasn't.

I simply lived beside the water.

I watched it.

Walked beside it.

Listened to it.

And slowly, something opened.

A sense that consciousness was larger than the visible world.

Some impressions felt deeply earthly.

The body.

The land.

The ancestors.

The tides.

The living world around me.

And others felt different.

More distant.

More spacious.

Almost as though they belonged to a field of consciousness beyond Earth.

I came to describe this as Pleiadian consciousness.

Not as something I can scientifically prove, but as something I genuinely experienced.

And the ocean became one of the places where this listening became strongest.

THE SKY WAS NEVER SEPARATE

The ancient Maya watched the heavens with extraordinary attention.

For them, the sky was not separate from life on Earth.

The movements of the celestial world belonged to a larger living order.

And the Caribbean holds another kind of mystery.

The sea reflects the stars.

The stars disappear into the horizon.

The reef continues beneath the surface.

Blue holes descend into darkness.

Caves open beneath the earth.

Water moves between what we can see and what we cannot.

And perhaps this is why the ocean has become such a powerful symbol of communication between worlds.

Not because we need to decide exactly what lies beyond.

But because standing before something this vast changes the way we listen.

The ocean quiets the noise.

And sometimes, when the noise becomes quiet enough, something else can be heard.
